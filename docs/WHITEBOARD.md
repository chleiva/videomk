# The whiteboard look (phase 1b, 2026-11-27)

**Since 2026-11-28** the look of a video is one decision (`docs/ART_DIRECTION.md`): on `auto` the whiteboard is one of the directions an art-direction step chooses from (it no longer has its own yes/no call), a chosen whiteboard look is enforced (at least 80 % boards, a real picture at most once per 30 s), and the board renderer is also the content panel beside cartoon characters (the `board` extra).


A whole video drawn and written by a marker on a whiteboard, in time with the narration. Library, handwriting and the standalone prototype are in `docs/WHITEBOARD_PHASE_1A_2026-11-27.md`; this page is how the look lives in the product engine. **Measured** = run on 2026-11-27, on QA Lambda where it says so, else on the owner's Mac. QA: on (commit 4beeb09). Production: off.

## How to ask for it
| Way | What happens |
|---|---|
| `"style": "whiteboard"` in `POST /users/{user}/videos/generate` | A whiteboard video, whatever the topic (the person asked). |
| Words in the prompt | EN: "as a whiteboard video", "whiteboard style", "draw it on a whiteboard", "hand-drawn video/style/explainer", "marker drawings", "doodle video", "sketch video". ES: "estilo pizarra", "en pizarra blanca", "vídeo de pizarra", "dibujado a mano", "dibujos a mano", "estilo boceto". Same result as the option. |
| `auto` (nothing sent) | The director may choose it only when the producer's kind of video is one of explainer-education, science-wonder, tutorial-howto, finance-business, health-wellbeing, kids-family AND one small model call agrees that the video explains a process or concept and needs no real pictures. Any doubt is no. |
| Never | Flag off; `style` cartoon or real; the words "real footage", "no whiteboard", "sin pizarra", "imágenes reales"; a video from the person's photos; a deck; on `auto`: current events (web research), news, biography, history, travel, product, true crime. |

Capabilities lists it under `looks.style.options`: `{"id": "whiteboard", "label": "Whiteboard" / "Pizarra", "note", "say": "as a whiteboard video" / "estilo pizarra", "available": <flag>}`. With the flag off the option is still accepted by the launcher and simply gives an ordinary video (as with `cartoon`).

## Flag and rights
* `WHITEBOARD=1` on the engine functions and on capabilities. `backend/stages.json`: `"whiteboard": true` for QA, absent for prod; or `-c whiteboard=1`. Needs `-c imageCatalog=1` (the deploy fails otherwise).
* The render and self-test functions get `s3:GetObject` on `w/*` of the catalog bucket and `CATALOG_BUCKET`. The planner needs nothing new: the library index ships with the code and the Cohere search uses the Bedrock right the plan function already has.
* No new function, route or other resource (a test compares the resource types of the stack with the flag on and off).

## How it works
1. **Policy** (`engine/whiteboard_policy.py`): `decide` (table above); `prompt_block` teaches the director the `whiteboard` scene and lists the 354 drawing ids by group; while the plan is cleaned a `whiteboard` scene is refused unless this video's policy allows it. The policy knows the video's aspect: a tall video (9:16, 4:5) gets its own layout advice (full-width titles, 1 to 3 words per line, top to bottom).
2. **The board the director writes** (`engine/scenes/elements/whiteboard.py`, hidden from the ordinary scene list): items in drawing order, never coordinates.
   * `draw` (library id), `write` (text; `^{2}`, `_{2}`, `\frac{a}{b}`, `->`), `chart` (`bars` or `line`, 2 to 6 values, labels: drawn by hand), `connect` (arrow, wavy, dashed, line), `mark` (underline, double_underline, circle, box, tick, cross, cross_out, highlight).
   * Each has a `zone` of a 3x3 grid (or `near` another item) and `say`: the narration word on which it is finished.
   * `"board": "same"` on a beat: it goes on drawing on the board of the beat before it (see 4).
3. **Validity, no model** (`sanitize`): at most 20 items and 9 drawings/texts/charts a beat; a drawing name is matched by id, then by exact title or synonym (EN/ES), then by meaning (Cohere Embed v4 against the bundled index, floor 0.30); **no match: the name is written in a box** (no image is ever generated in the request path); arrows and marks that point at nothing are dropped; an empty board is refused and the beat falls back. Text beats the director wrote as title, bullets, stat or quote are rewritten as boards (`enforce`).
4. **Boards are full pictures shared by beats** (`compose`, when the script is final). One beat (about 10 s, 20 to 25 words) cannot fill a board, so consecutive beats share one: the director marks the continuing beat, and the engine also keeps a beat on the board when the board is not full yet (fewer than 8 things or 40 words), up to 3 beats, 12 drawings and texts, 66 words. A thing whose zone is taken moves to a free zone of the same shape; a beat that finds no free zone opens a new board. The closing beat stays on the last board. Result on the three test prompts: 2 to 4 boards of 15 to 21 s with 12 to 18 items each (before: 5 to 8 boards of 5 items).
5. **When the narration is final** (`settle`, last step of the director): `compose`; `retie` reorders items into the order the narrator names them and moves a tie whose word was cut; `keep_busy` adds an emphasis mark, tied to a word, wherever 5 or more words pass with nothing drawn (a thing gets at most ONE emphasis mark; when everything is marked the pen rests). Then the **self-check** (below).
6. **Timeline** (`whiteboard_policy.link`, called by `engine/timeline.py`): the scenes of one board know each other (`before`: what earlier beats drew, there from the first frame; `after`: what later beats will draw, their place is kept); the first scene of a later board carries the previous board (`prev`: the camera travels from it), the last scene carries all (`wall`). Items get board-wide names (`0:sun`), so a thing looks the same in every scene of its board. Here the REAL word times are known, so `fill_rests` adds emphasis marks at fixed times wherever the pen would rest more than 1.6 s: between two tied things, at the start of a beat that continues a board, and in the breath after the last word (at most 3 a scene, on things of this board that have no mark yet; never a second mark on a thing). The library build date is stamped into the props (segment hash).
7. **Render** (`engine/whiteboard/spec.py`, `board.py`): layout, timing and ONE framing per board.
   * **Layout**: rows and columns share the board by what they hold (a title row is lower than a row of drawings; an empty row or column gives up most of its room). A drawing fills its zone less the room of its labels. Handwriting is never under 76 board units (4.7 % of the frame's shorter side on screen): text that does not fit spreads into free neighbouring zones, a number or short phrase is written smaller on one line rather than wrapped, the rest wraps. A chart takes a free zone above or below and its numbers have a minimum size.
   * **Arrows and emphasis marks** are fitted to the real ink of what is on the board and never touch anything: see "Marks and arrows" below.
   * **Timing**: each item ends 0.15 s after its word starts and never later when that is possible: items before a squeezed one are finished sooner (backward pass), then the pen goes up to 1.6x faster. A drawing with idle time before it is drawn up to 2.6x slower, a written block is started earlier and written up to 1.8x slower. Nothing is drawn while the camera arrives.
   * **Camera**: the whole board (everything it will ever hold, with a margin), enlarged up to 1.45x when the board is not full; still for every scene of the board. It moves only between boards (0.8 s) and for the final overview: the boards' content boxes side by side inside the content box of the frame, above the captions (3.8 s: 1.5 s travel, then held before the 1.4 s closing fade). With more than 4 boards the overview would be too small to read: the video stays on the last board and only glances at the whole (1.8 s).
   The board is the content box of `engine/frame.py` (above the caption band, below a top watermark band) for all four aspects. The theme is `paper`; no moving background.
8. **Memory and cache**: one built board per container plus small finished pictures of other boards (at most 10). Drawing packages: `s3://<catalog>/w/<id>.json`, kept under `/tmp/whiteboard` and at most 48 in memory. `WHITEBOARD_DIR` points tests and laptops at a folder instead.
9. **Never a failed render**: a missing package is a boxed label; a board that cannot be built is a plain card with its first written line.

## Self-check (`engine/whiteboard/score.py`, `whiteboard_policy.review`)
After `settle` every board is laid out and timed exactly as the renderer will, without drawing a pixel and without the drawing packages (shape and drawing time of each object come from the bundled index; word times from the measured speaking rate). Per board: things per 10 s (at least 4, never under 4 on a board of 10 s or more), coverage of the framed picture by the boxes of drawings and writing (at least 30 %), smallest letter size (at least 4.4 % of the shorter side), overlaps, things off the board, `collisions` (a mark or an arrow that touches something, on every board), arrows that found no clean way (`blocked`: shortened to a stub or left out), pen-busy share (at least 45 %), longest rest (at most 2.2 s with estimated times), items finished more than 0.8 s after their word. A board that fails is sent back to the director ONCE with its complaints in plain words ("only 4 drawings and written phrases for about 19 s: it needs at least 6 ... in the free zones (left, bottom)"); the narration is never changed; the new boards are kept only if the video then scores at least as well. **Overlaps are never shipped (2026-11-27, languages-qa-fixes).** Three rules, after the first Polish video showed two beats written on top of each other: (1) `compose` knows the aspect and lays a shared board out as the renderer will (`score.defects`): a beat shares a board only if its things really fit there, else it gets its own board with the zones its author gave it (`split_for_room`); a free zone alone is not room. (2) When the rewritten boards are kept, the WHOLE trial is taken, not the rewritten beats plus the others' `board` flags (that was the bug: a beat got the flag `same` but kept the zones of a board of its own, and the score had been taken on the trial, so it said `overlaps: 0`). (3) Last resort, whatever the repair did (`make_safe`, `untangle`): a board whose drawings, texts or charts still overlap or leave the board loses its last beat to a fresh board, then things move to a free zone, get one size smaller, and at the very end are taken off, until the renderer's own layout finds nothing (logged as pass `safe`, kept in `score.made_safe`). The summary counts `overlaps` and `off_board` on every board, failing and shared ones included; `not_laid_out` counts boards without numbers. **A mark or arrow that touches something is never shipped either** (2026-11-27, whiteboard-marks-fix): the layout guarantees it ("Marks and arrows"), and `make_clean` is the plan's own last check of it (pass `clean`; it finds nothing unless the layout fails). **A board that still "looks empty"** after the rewrite is filled by what is on it (`fill_out`, pass `fill`): one thing at a time, written texts first, is made one size larger and kept only if the renderer's layout then covers more, with nothing overlapping, off the board, written smaller or lost; it stops at the bar (the Polish and Portuguese boards that shipped at 28 % reach 33 % and 30 %). Boards that miss only the count of things ("7 for 20 s, needs 8") or the pacing bar are still shipped as they are: see "What a failing board does" below. Logged as `WHITEBOARD_SCORE {...}` (pass 1 and pass 2) by the plan function and kept in `plan.whiteboard.score` (`first`, `second`, `kept`, `boards_detail`). Cost: one more call of the script model when a board fails (about USD 0.05 to 0.08 and 25 to 40 s of planning).

## Marks and arrows (2026-11-27, whiteboard-marks-fix; `engine/whiteboard/spec.py`)
Six QA videos showed circles that swallowed the first letter of the next label ("bliksem" read "liksem"), circles that cut the phrase they surrounded, an underline AND a box on one word, ovals running into the drawing beside them, and arrows through a water drop and a calendar, while the score said `overlaps 0`. Causes: (1) a mark was sized from the ROOM a line of text was given, not from its ink (the tails of j, p, g, y, ę hang 20 to 40 units below that box, a leading j starts 19 units left of it), and a circle was an ellipse through the middle of the sides of that box, which cuts its corners, that is the first and last letters; (2) a mark never looked at what stands beside its target; (3) marks were not part of any measure; (4) an arrow took the least bad of eight bends even when all of them crossed something, and the board shipped with `crossed 1`.
* **Real ink.** Every written word has an ink box from the pen path over its letters plus half the stroke (`word_inks`; checked in the tests against the rasterised letters, accents and tails included: never short, at most 20 units generous). A drawing is its box.
* **Order.** What the board holds is placed first (zone things, then things beside them), then the marks, then the arrows, then whatever depends on those; a last sweep takes off anything that still touches (`_collisions`), so the result is zero by construction.
* **A mark** is drawn 30 (else 22) units outside the ink it marks, measured from its centre line, and after the hand's wobble no point of it may be nearer than 15 units to its own ink, 20 to any other ink, 30 to another mark or arrow. A circle round writing is a rounded loop that holds the whole padded rectangle; round a drawing a true ellipse (its own drawing is taken as its box less the corners); a drawing with a label beside it is circled together with the label. It may run up to 70 units (sides), 50 (top), 34 (bottom) into the paper round the board. No room for a circle, box or double underline: an underline (a little shorter when something stands right beside the word). No room for that: no mark. **One mark per thing**: a second one is left out. A mark the engine itself added for pacing (`em<n>_...`, `+r...`) moves to the latest free thing drawn before it instead of being lost.
* **An arrow** starts and ends outside its two things and the marks on them, and no point of it comes within 24 units of any other ink or mark: the straight way or the gentlest bend; else a way round the corners of what is between (the shortest first, 16 units accepted when the way is narrow); else a stub of 90 to 170 units that leaves its start towards the target or arrives at it; else no arrow. Two things that nearly touch (under 44 units of free way) get no arrow, and a short arrow is never bent hard. An arrow wins over the marks on its own two ends: if they leave it no direct way they are taken off.
* **Measure.** The layout report has `collisions` ([[mark or arrow, what it touches]]: a stroke within 11 units of ink, or two strokes within 19), `marks` (kept, degraded, dropped, moved) and `arrows` (direct, rerouted, stub, dropped). The score counts them on every board; a blocked arrow is a complaint to the director ("connect neighbours"). `compile_board(..., tidy=False)` lays a board out as it used to be drawn, only to measure a plan as written.
* **Measured on ten real videos' boards** (five of the six QA videos of 2026-11-27 plus three English and two Spanish earlier ones; fixtures in `backend/tests/fixtures/whiteboard_boards`): collisions 149 before (58 of them a mark on its own target), 0 after. Of 105 marks 52 are drawn as asked, 38 as an underline, 15 left out; of 40 arrows 26 direct, 2 routed round, 5 stubs, 7 left out. Before and after pictures: `~/Downloads/videomk-looks/web/wbmarks_*_before.jpg`, `_after.jpg`.
* **Cost of it.** Fewer marks means the pen rests a little more: on the five plans the longest rest (estimated times) went from 1.2 to 1.8 s to 1.3 to 2.8 s and pen busy from 0.53 to 0.63 to 0.53 to 0.62.

## What a failing board does (fullness and pacing)
Today: a board that fails the self-check is sent back to the director once; whatever comes back, overlaps (`make_safe`), touching marks and arrows (layout, `make_clean`) and a board that "looks empty" (`fill_out`) are repaired without a model. A board that has too few things for its length ("7 drawings and written phrases for 20 s, needs 8"), a pen that rests too long or is busy too little is SHIPPED with its complaint in `plan.whiteboard.score.boards_detail`. Proposed, not built: (1) the count bar and the coverage bar say the same thing two ways, so a board one thing short whose coverage is 40 % or more should pass (the Polish board 3 had 45 %); (2) a board that is two or more things short gets written labels beside its unlabelled drawings from the library title in the video's language (deterministic, but it needs the translated titles, which exist for en and es only); (3) a rest over the bar gets a mark from `fill_rests` on a thing chosen by ROOM (the layout can say which things have room: `marks.moved` already does it for marks that did not fit) instead of "the latest thing". A second model call is not proposed: it costs USD 0.05 to 0.08 and 25 to 40 s and the first one already failed at the same request.

## Costs
Per video: the director's usual calls plus about 14,000 characters of prompt (rules and the id menu); at most one small call for `auto`; one more call of the script model when the self-check fails a board; one Cohere embedding only for a drawing name not found by name. The library cost USD 4.90 once.

## Limits and known weak points (looked at the QA videos of the final build)
* **How full a board is still depends on the director.** When it plans boards in pairs (most 16:9 runs) a board has 12 to 18 items like the demos; when it writes each beat as its own full-zone board, the nine zones leave no room to merge and boards have 8 items. The self-check only catches boards under its bar.
* **Tall videos (9:16, 4:5)** are the weakest: the grid is 3x3 in every aspect, so a tall board has wide gaps between its rows and the final overview is small. A 2-column, 4-row grid for tall frames is the next step.
* Emphasis marks added for the pen's rests land on the latest unmarked thing with room, not on the most important word. On a crowded board many circles become underlines (also under drawings), and an arrow between things far apart can end as a stub that points the right way but stops short.
* A drawing is known to the layout by its box, not by its strokes (the score has no drawing packages): a loop round a drawing clears the box less its corners, so ink in the very corner of a drawing's box could still be met by its own circle.
* A re-zoned title can end up in the middle of a board. Charts are small in a single zone (they take a second zone only when it is free).
* The library has 354 objects: a topic outside it gets stand-ins (cheese for wax cells) or written words.
* Pen busy measured 77 to 79 % with pen travel counted, above the 50 to 70 % aimed at; counting only tenths with new ink it is 63 to 66 %.
* The whiteboard look is never chosen by itself when the person asked for a presenter character (and a presenter never appears on a whiteboard video).
* Only Latin handwriting: every letter of en, es, fr, de, it, pt, nl, pl, fi has pen strokes (tables rebuilt with `scripts/whiteboard_proto/glyph_table.py`, which needs SciPy; the engine does not). A sign outside the tables is written from the font with a plain left-to-right pen path, not a traced centre line. Ties are one word. The editor apps have no board editor (editing the narration re-ties the board). 4K not re-measured. 1:1 and 4:5 were looked at as local renders only.

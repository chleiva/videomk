# Your own photos in a video (first slice, 2026-11-23)

Up to **100 photos** per video. In the app: tap the photo button in the composer (system photo picker: no permission prompt, the app only gets the photos
you tap), say what the video should be about, send. The row in the list shows the upload, then the usual progress, then the video.

## Flow
1. **Phone** (`PhotoPrep`): each photo is made upright, at most 2048 px on its longest side, and re-encoded with **no metadata at all** (no GPS, camera or date). Tested with real EXIF/GPS. Before that, `PhotoMetaReader` reads each original's capture time and GPS (rounded to 2 decimals, about 1 km) into `media_meta` of the generate request only, unless the Settings switches 'Use photo dates' / 'Use photo locations' are off; the uploaded JPEG never carries them. Up to **100 photos** per video; they are prepared a few at a time and uploaded four at a time with retries (iPhone: system photo picker; Android: a chooser that lists the phone's gallery app, Google Photos and Files).
2. **Upload** (`POST /users/{u}/uploads`, then S3): one presigned POST per photo, pinned to one key in the user's own folder (`uploads/<user>/<id>.jpg`), `image/jpeg`, at most 10 MB. 600 photos per user per day.
3. **Request**: `POST .../generate` with `media: [ids]` (at most 100). The launcher checks every id is well formed and really exists in the caller's folder before anything is charged. Credits are unchanged (1 per started minute).
4. **Prepare** (`engine/usermedia.py`, in parallel): re-reads each upload, rejects anything that is not a photo (GIF, bomb, tiny, unreadable), rotates, strips everything, writes a clean copy under `generation/v2/<video>/assets/user_P<n>.jpg` (numbered in the order given), and **deletes the original**.
5. **Catalogue** (`engine/curate.py`): the pictures are shown to a model as few times as possible. Photos are laid on **contact sheets of 10** (a 5x2 grid, each cell numbered in its corner), and a small vision model describes every numbered photo: caption, recognisable place, subject, people or not, quality, near-duplicates on the sheet, and the safety verdict. One image per 10 photos, in parallel; 100 photos = 10 images.
6. **Story** (`engine/curate.py`): one text-only call to the writing model reads the person's request and the numbered captions and works out what the photos are about and what the video is for, the voice (first person for "my holiday", a narrator otherwise), a tone, chapters, and **moments** (1 photo, or 2 to 5 that belong together and are shown as a gallery), in an order that tells the story, plus the photos to leave out (duplicates, blurred, surplus). The length caps how many photos are used (about 3.5 s each over 80 % of the video: 41 for 3 minutes, 109 for 8). `reconcile` makes the answer safe: only real ids, each placed once, 1 to 5 per moment, the model's own left-out decisions kept, the best photos kept when over the cap (poor quality and near-duplicates go first, the first and last stay), a mechanical grouping if the model fails.
7. **Script**: the director never sees a picture. It gets the story, the voice and the moments with the photo ids and captions, and writes **one beat per moment** (an `image` scene for a single photo, a `gallery` scene `{photos: [...]}` for a group), with chapter titles from the story, and may add non-photo beats (a map, a fact about a landmark from the research) where the story needs context. Every photo of every moment must be used (one retry, then the missing ones are added).
8. **Render**: `image` and `gallery` scenes of the user's photos; landscape photos fill the frame, portrait and square photos are shown whole over a dark blurred copy of themselves (never a face cropped off), in galleries too. Each photo of a gallery appears when the narration reaches its caption.

## Photo date and place (2026-11-24)
Two switches in Settings (both on by default): **Use photo dates** and **Use photo locations**. Before the metadata is stripped, the phone reads the capture time (EXIF DateTimeOriginal, local wall-clock, no timezone) and the GPS position and sends them in the generate request as `media_meta: {media id: {taken, lat, lon}}`, with lat and lon **rounded to 2 decimals (about 1 km) on the phone**. Never inside the JPEG, never in a log. The launcher (`common/photo_ids.valid_media_meta`) accepts only those three fields, well-formed, for ids that are in `media`, and rounds again. The engine then: numbers the photos in the order they were taken when at least half have a time (`usermedia.prepare`); names each photo's place from the bundled Natural Earth populated places, offline ("Paris, France", "near Versailles, France"; `engine/geoname.py`), so no third party sees a location; **forgets the coordinates** (`usermedia.attach_places`); gives the story model each photo's time and place and a trip summary ("taken 11 to 14 Apr 2026, 4 days; places from the GPS: Paris, Versailles"), telling it to trust them over visual guesses for order and place names and to start a new chapter when the day changes. Only the place name and the time stay in the plan (`media_items`). iPhone gives original data through the photo picker (dates and GPS); on Android the system photo picker and MediaStore redact GPS unless the app holds ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION (not requested), so expect dates for most photos and places for a minority (photos reached through gallery apps in the chooser often keep GPS).

## Cost (estimated from the model prices in `engine/llm.py`; check with `scripts/cost_report.py` after a real run)
For 100 photos: catalogue about 10 contact sheets x (about 1,000 image tokens + 600 text in, 900 out) on the small model, about **$0.006**; story about 5,500 tokens in and 3,000 out on the writing model, about **$0.04**; total about **$0.05**, the same order as the script itself. The cost of the catalogue grows with the number of sheets and the story with the number of captions, so it stays small. Nothing re-sends the pictures after the catalogue.

## Safety and privacy
- Safety review (`PHOTO_REVIEW=on|log_only|off`): nudity, graphic violence, identity documents / bank cards / screens with personal data, hate symbols are left out (the video is made from the rest; if none is usable the request is declined with a reason). Ordinary photos of people, children, family and pets are fine. A failed review never blocks a video. It is a cheap filter, not a legal safeguard.
- Photos go only to Bedrock inside our AWS account. Originals are deleted as soon as the clean copy exists (a lifecycle rule removes any leftover after 2 days). The clean copies stay with the video (editing needs them) and must be deleted with the video and with the account (account deletion is still to build).
- The privacy policy must say: photos are processed by an AI model to write and make the video, location data is removed, originals are deleted, copies are kept with the video until it is deleted.

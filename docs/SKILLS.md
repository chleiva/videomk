# Craft guides ("skills") for the director (2026-11-24)

A *producer* step reads the request, picks the one or two guides that fit the kind of video, and the director follows them while it writes. Two small always-on guides, **Ten principles of an outstanding video** and **Publish quality**, go into every video, and a **platform guide** (from the create option `platform`) is added when a platform was chosen. Code: `engine/skills.py`; guides: `engine/skills/*.md` (plain markdown, under 1,400 characters each, imperative bullets).

## How it works
1. `director.direct` makes one cheap call (the small model, about 120 output tokens) with the guide catalogue (each guide's `pick:` line) and the request; it answers `{"types": [...]}`. At most two ids, never the always-on one, invented ids dropped, the default (`explainer-education`) if the model fails or names nothing valid. A failure never stops a video.
2. The chosen guides plus Publish quality are appended to the director's system prompt, and to the outline and every section prompt of a long video (`craft=`). Which guides were used is stored as `plan["skills"]` and in the research info, for review.
3. Guides refine the general craft rules already in the prompt (hook, one idea per beat, pauses with reasons); they add what is specific to a kind of video. Their "visuals" lines name scene types, and the intro tells the director to use one only if it is in the scene list.

## Platform and common guides (2026-11-26)
Research and evidence: `docs/PLATFORM_SKILLS_RESEARCH.md`. Three layers, rendered in this order by `skills.render(ids, platform)`:
1. **Common (always on):** `outstanding-video` (ten principles: payoff first, one promise kept, open question, concrete, show what you say, vary pace, cut, sound-off and ear, trust, one takeaway) and `publish-quality`. Cap 1,600 characters each (`MAX_ALWAYS_CHARS`).
2. **Platform (deterministic):** `platform-<id>.md` for `youtube`, `shorts`, `tiktok`, `reels`, `instagram-feed`, `linkedin`, `x`, `other`. The file header carries `platform: <create-option id>` (with an underscore, e.g. `instagram_feed`) and `auto: true`, so the producer never sees it. `director.direct` reads `options["platform"]` and calls `skills.render(skill_ids, platform)`; an unknown or missing platform adds nothing. Cap 1,700 characters (`MAX_PLATFORM_CHARS`). Switch: server setting `PLATFORM_SKILLS`, **default off** (`1`/`true`/`on` enables, see `skills.platform_skills_enabled`): the A/B of 2026-11-26 (below) found no clear gain, so the guides ship dormant.
3. **Content (picked):** the one or two guides the producer chooses.
They add to, never replace, the framing line (`plan.frame_prompt`: aspect, platform length and style, SHORT-FORM block for videos under a minute, the creator's brief). The platform guides state concrete safe-zone fractions of the frame for the planner's own on-screen text; the burned-in captions are placed by `engine/frame.py` (see the gaps in the research doc, section 3).
To add or change a platform guide: edit `engine/skills/platform-<id>.md`, keep it as imperative bullets under the cap, and run `tests/test_skills.py` (it checks that every id in `format_options.PLATFORMS` has a guide, caps, order and determinism). A new platform id in `format_options.PLATFORMS` needs its file or the test fails.

## The guides
Always on: `outstanding-video`, `publish-quality`. Platform: see above. Picked by type: `explainer-education`, `news-explainer`, `history-story`, `tutorial-howto`, `coding-tutorial`, `product-marketing`, `inspiration-motivation`, `funny-light`, `travel-places`, `science-wonder`, `mystery-true-crime`, `biography-profile`, `listicle-ranking`, `finance-business`, `health-wellbeing`, `kids-family`, `comparison-versus`, `recap-summary`.
To add one: drop a `.md` file in `engine/skills/` with `id`, `name` and a `pick` line; the test suite checks its shape.

## What each guide stands on (honest sourcing)
The web research mostly returned vendor and creator blogs, with little primary material, so the guides combine three kinds of ground; none of the numbers is presented as a measurement.
- **Peer-reviewed learning research.** Mayer's multimedia principles (signalling, coherence, segmenting, personalisation, spoken words over printed) underpin the educational, tutorial, coding, kids and science guides: one idea per beat, only essential text on screen, conversational language, define a term after its idea, show the example ([NUS summary](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/medixperience/scholarly-article/mayers-12-principles-of-multimedia-instruction/), [Cambridge Handbook](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-managing-essential-processing-in-multimedia-learning/A9E77D0172F905AC957689D1771E2888)).
- **Creator and producer practice, as reported.** Vox frames a topic as a question with a promised payoff and plants ideas early to expand later; Johnny Harris writes in active language that shows who did what, which gives the picture something to show; the curiosity gap has to stay open and close inside the video, ideally closing one while opening another ([Descript](https://descript.com/blog/article/the-youtubers-guide-to-the-curiosity-gap-how-to-keep-your-audiences-interest), [Recapio on Vox](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-write-a-video-like-vox-by-k-j-grey), [Medium on Harris](https://medium.com/@LMK_writing/how-johnny-harris-mastered-visual-storytelling-on-youtube-343ddf9160ec)). The format taxonomy (listicle, explainer, commentary, interview, narrative, ...) comes from [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2019/02/12/taxonomy-youtube-videos-develop-original-content-that-works/); how-to videos open by stating what the video is about and why it is worth watching ([HeyGen](https://community.heygen.com/en/public/resources/heygen-academy-foundations-study-format)); product explainers state the problem and how the product solves it ([TechSmith](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/explainer-video/)). Writing for the ear (conversational, short sentences, active voice, read it aloud, avoid hard-to-say numbers and acronyms) is repeated across voiceover guides ([The eLearning Coach](https://theelearningcoach.com/elearning_design/the-art-of-writing-great-voice-over-scripts/), [Artlist](https://artlist.io/blog/how-to-write-voice-over-scripts/)).
- **Established craft that no single page proves.** The news inverted pyramid and spoken attribution (standard journalism practice), problem-agitate-solve and benefits-before-features (marketing copywriting), the story arc used for history, biography and inspiration (want, obstacle, turning point, change), the rule of three and understatement for humour, evidence-strength language for health, and the safety rules (no accusation of private people in true-crime, no personal financial or medical advice, balanced treatment of contested topics) are long-standing professional norms, written here as short instructions rather than cited findings.

## Publish quality: what the guide asks and what the engine enforces
- **The guide asks** (the director's part): accuracy and attribution, a specific title that pays off, descriptive chapter titles, key terms on screen for sound-off viewers, jargon defined, no filler, one takeaway at the end, respect and balance.
- **The engine enforces** (checked by tests): the video is never shorter than the length asked, the final audio is levelled to **-14 LUFS integrated, true peak -1.5 dB** (what YouTube plays at; sources agree on -14, with -16 and -12 to -10 as outliers for other platforms; set `TARGET_LUFS` to change), music ducks under the voice, captions are on unless turned off, only licensed music from the library is used.
- **Not done:** thumbnails, platform-specific cuts (9:16 Shorts), end screens and uploading. Sources cite 1080x1920 and under 60 seconds for Shorts, but I found nothing authoritative to base a vertical edit on.

## Not measured
Nothing here has been tested against real viewers: there is no analytics in the app. These are informed defaults to be tuned with watch-time data and with the testers' feedback.


## A/B of the platform guides (2026-11-26, QA, USD 11.14 of the 15 cap)
Script `backend/scripts/platform_ab.py`; results in `docs/audit/2026-11-26-platform-skills/` (`off/`, `on/`, `legacy/`, `pair*/`, `summary*.json`, `spend.log`). 10 prompts x 5 platforms (youtube 2 min, tiktok 30 s, reels 30 s, linkedin 1 min, x 45 s), the real director, no web agent, research notes and picked content guides shared by both arms so only the platform guide differs; a deterministic platform rubric plus a blind pairwise model judge (Sonnet 5.5, position randomised).
- **Platform guide on vs off:** the blind judge preferred ON in youtube 6-4, x 6-4, linkedin 4-6; for tiktok and reels the first run was ON 5-5 and 7-3, and after one wording fix to the ending lines (tuned on the same prompts) the rerun was 3-7 and 2-8. Same prompts, opposite verdicts: sampling noise at n=10 per platform is as large as any effect. Total last run 21 for ON, 29 for OFF; first run 28 to 22. The rubric moved by a few points either way (hook_ok, text density, beat length, length fit); on-screen words per beat fell slightly on reels, tiktok, linkedin; cost per plan +3% to +10% on the short platforms, -5% on youtube; latency within 1 s. **Conclusion: no clear gain, so `PLATFORM_SKILLS` stays off.** A stronger test needs more prompts or real viewing data.
- **New always-on guides vs the old Publish quality alone (youtube, tiktok, linkedin; 30 pairs):** deterministic hook check passed 1.0 vs 0.7-0.8 (the first spoken sentence is short, not a greeting, not a title card), checks_passed up on youtube and tiktok; the blind judge was a wash (14 vs 16). Plan cost +3% to +11%. These guides are always on (not behind the flag) because they do no harm and fix the opening in the cheap metric; flip them back by restoring `publish-quality.md` and deleting `outstanding-video.md`.
- Engine limit seen in the data: short videos get about 3 beats per 30 s (`plan.SHORT_BEAT_S`), so beats stay near 20 words however a guide asks for shorter ones.
- Alignment with the quality builder's engagement block (`engine/engagement.py`, flag `ENGAGEMENT_PROMPT`, default off): both ask for a concrete hook, one through-line, concrete before general and no filler or CTA; the engagement block adds banned phrases and cadence. If both are switched on they overlap (about 400 extra prompt tokens) but do not contradict; `docs/QUALITY_REVIEW.md` did not exist when this was written, so re-check it.

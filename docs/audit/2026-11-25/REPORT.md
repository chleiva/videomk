# Quality audit of the current plan stage (2026-11-25)

Scope: the PLAN stage only (`director.direct` exactly as `plan_handler` wires it: web provider + `ask_tools`, Haiku for small calls and the research agent, Sonnet 5.5 for the script), no TTS, no images, no render. 40 prompts, 17 categories, 12 in Spanish, 1 to 5 minutes, QA account, `eu-west-1`. Production code was not changed. Raw results: `runs/*.json` (one per prompt: prompt, options, research info, notes, plan, warnings, timings, cost, rules, judge). Aggregates: `tables.md`, `stats` are regenerated by `backend/scripts/audit_analyze.py`. The human sheet is `review.html` (`review.md` as fallback).

**Spend: about $7.5 of the $12 cap** (plan stage $6.36 for the 40 final runs (6.36) + $0.28 for one crashed attempt, judge $1.04, about $0.1 for two re-judgements). Tavily searches counted at $0.008 each ($0.86 total).

How to read the numbers: "rules" are deterministic and cheap; the "judge" is Claude Sonnet 5.5 reading the script and the research notes (strict anchors, must quote the line for each defect). The judge is harsh on purpose (0 of 40 scripts "would publish", mean 3.28/5): use it to **rank categories and find defects**, not as an absolute score. It has not been calibrated against the owner yet; the human sheet exists for that (see Limitations).

## 1. Headline

* The pipeline never produces a broken or off-topic video (39 of 40 first attempts produced a plan (the 40th succeeded on a retry); language was right 40/40; hooks are good; scene variety is good). It produces **competent but shallow and generic** scripts: lowest judge dimensions are **originality 2.48** and **coverage/depth 2.80**; accuracy 3.15; tone 3.90 and language 4.05 are fine.
* The dominant cause is **research input**, not writing: in 32 of 40 videos the encyclopedia path was used, and **16 of 40 ran with under 1,500 characters of notes (15 with no source at all)**; the median video that used Wikipedia cited **1 page**. Some of those empty results were HTTP 429 from Wikimedia that `gather_notes` swallows silently (observed directly, see 3.1).
* Videos of 4 to 5 minutes (staged, with per-section research) score better on coverage (3.11 vs 2.71 for 1-3 minutes), originality (2.89 vs 2.35-2.36) and have far fewer ungrounded numbers/names (8% vs 21-25%). Same writer model, so the difference points at **outline + per-section research**, which today only kicks in at 4.0 minutes. (Confounded by category and length: the 4-5 minute prompts were history, biography, science, finance, travel, kids.)
* The most severe problems are **invented specifics** (product features in marketing scripts, a lap number and a penalty in the F1 recap, a study named from memory in a health script) and **length overrun on short videos** (1-2 minute scripts run 18-23% over target).

### 3.1 Thin or missing research on the encyclopedia path (most frequent, root cause of 3.3)
* Evidence: 16/40 videos with notes under 1,500 chars, 15/40 with no source cited (bio-01, fic-02, fin-01, hea-01, kid-01, mkt-01/02, opi-01/02, pro-01/02, rec-01, sci-01, tri-03 had **0 chars**; how-01 had 356; tec-01 had 980). Median Wikipedia pages per video: 1. Judge coverage on these: mean 2.7; typical defects: `rec-01` "First, soften the onions and peppers in oil" (no quantities, heat or times: a viewer cannot cook from it), `pro-01` the long break after four rounds never gets a duration, `tri-01` "Fact five. You are built from water, protein, fats, carbohydrates and DNA" (a textbook statement, not a surprising fact; the facts came from a Wikipedia page about cell counts), `tec-01` never mentions tokens or human feedback (RLHF).
* Measured side finding: Wikimedia answers `429 Too Many Requests` (with `retry-after: 3`) to bursts from one IP; `research.gather_notes` catches every `FetchError` and returns empty notes with no log, no retry, no fallback. When I re-ran the same queries, some produced notes and some got 429 (e.g. `sci-01`: 0 chars in the audit, 5,465 chars when retried; `hea-01`, `opi-01`, `pro-01` still 429). I cannot separate how many of the 15 zero-note runs were 429s and how many were genuinely empty search results, because the audit did not log fetch errors (I did not change production code). The audit ran 3 prompts in parallel from one laptop IP, and some of my own debugging bursts may have worsened the limit, so treat the 429 share as unknown; in production the Lambdas share NAT addresses, so the risk is real. `notes_recheck.json` has the re-test.
* Cause: `research.gather_notes` (2-3 Wikipedia pages max, en/es only, `exchars=2600`, errors swallowed, `netsafe.USER_AGENT = "explainer-engine/2.0"` is a generic agent that Wikimedia rate-limits harder than an identifying one); `researcher.decide` routes only "news-like" topics to the web, so how-to, recipes, products, health guidance, opinions with evidence, travel and comparisons stay on Wikipedia.
* Fix: (a) log and count fetch failures, retry once after `retry-after`, and send a descriptive User-Agent with contact (value H, effort 0.5 day, risk none); (b) when the encyclopedia path returns under ~1,500 chars, fall back to the web agent (already exists, `info["fallback"]` path) (H, 0.5 day); (c) widen `decide` so practical/how-to/health/finance/travel/comparison prompts use web (M-H, 1 day, costs about $0.05-0.10 per video).

### 3.2 Invented or unsupported specifics (most severe)
* Evidence: judge accuracy defects: 79 (25 major, 0 critical); web-research videos scored accuracy **2.88**, lower than encyclopedia ones (3.22), because the writer adds details around real notes. Examples (quotes from the narration):
  * `new-01` (F1 recap): "despite a ten-second penalty" and "Lap 47" are not in the notes; "Sergio Perez and Alex Albon also did not finish" (notes say Albon did not start); the closing gives Qatar dates and venue that are not in the notes; judge accuracy 2.
  * `mkt-02` (note-taking app, vague brief): "Jotly works offline too, and catches up the moment you reconnect." and "Jotly suggests tags and folders for you." Features invented as facts; `mkt-01`: "There are no contracts and no awkward phone calls." (policy never given).
  * `hea-03`: "In one Harvard study, people who said 'I am excited' spoke more persuasively" and "A meta-analysis of 25 trials found CBT for social anxiety..." (not in the notes; the second figure cannot be traced).
  * `cmp-02`: "Tiempo mínimo para que el eléctrico compense, según la calculadora" (the 4-5 years came from a list of conditions, not a calculator); `his-02`: "El 18, Huerta firma el Pacto de la Ciudadela" (it was the Pacto de la Embajada); `sci-03`: "It already treats disease in one patient" (Casgevy is approved for two diseases).
  * Source-leak into the narration: `how-02` "Según las notas, la buena forma física nace de ejercicio moderado..." and `fin-03` "On the loan in our notes, interest alone runs about $684 a month" (2/40 videos talk about the notes aloud; the second also uses a loan the viewer never saw).
* Rule view: ungrounded numbers/names average 20% (en 24%, es 10%); by design an upper bound (known facts count), and its correlation with the judge's accuracy is r = 0.05, i.e. it is a triage flag, not a score. Where there were no notes (14 videos) the model mostly avoided numbers (no_notes runs: only bio-01 had 7 dates from memory, mostly right).
* Cause: no claim-level check after writing; `sources_used` is self-reported (`director.direct` keeps whatever titles the model lists); marketing prompts get no instruction to use only given facts (the `product-marketing` skill is not enough for vague briefs); scene docs say "only numbers in the notes" but nothing enforces it.
* Fix: (a) post-write grounding check on numbers and named entities in beats vs notes + prompt, one repair call naming the ungrounded items, only for time-sensitive/factual categories (the V3 item 3; my `quality_audit.grounding()` is a ready prototype) (H, 3 days); (b) a hard rule in the system prompt and the marketing/health/finance skills: "never state a feature, price, policy, study or statistic that is not in the notes or the brief; never mention 'the notes'" plus a regex that strips beats containing "the notes/las notas/our notes" (H, 0.5 day).

### 3.3 Shallow coverage and low originality on 1-3 minute videos
* Evidence: coverage 2.71 and originality 2.36 for 1-3 minute videos (4-5 minute: 3.11 and 2.89). Worst categories: trivia 2.83, travel 2.89, biography 3.00, news 3.11, opinion 3.11, recipe 3.11. Examples: `bio-03` (Turing, 2 min) spends a whole beat on "Cloudflare reported in 2021 that the average person needs about 32 seconds to solve a CAPTCHA" while the 1952 prosecution, the pardon and Bletchley's stakes are missing; `tra-01` gives all of day three one sentence ("Day three, escape to Sintra...") and teases a yellow tram in the hook that is never explained; `cmp-01` (React vs Vue) has no jobs or learning-curve data, "Pick React if you want wide, common usage" is vague; `opi-01` "Research results vary, and studies disagree on productivity" names no study and the conclusion is hedged although the request asked for a clear one.
* Cause: V3 hypothesis 3 is confirmed in direction: `staged.is_staged` starts at 4.0 minutes; shorter videos are written in one Sonnet call, no outline, only the whole-topic notes (3.1). Also `explainer-education` is the default skill for 14/40 videos, a generic guide.
* Fix: lower the staged threshold (try 2.0-2.5 minutes with 2-3 sections) or add the "planner lite" outline for 2-4 minutes (V3 item 5) (M-H, 4-6 days); combined with 3.1 this is probably the biggest quality gain. Cheaper first test: apply 3.1 only and re-run this audit (cost about $7) before building any planner.

### 3.4 Short videos run over length
* Evidence: median estimated speech time vs requested: 1 min 1.23x, 2 min 1.18x, 3 min 1.04x, 4-5 min 1.12x (max 1.44x: `mkt-01` 194 words for a 134-word target, `fic-01` 379 vs 267). `too_long` flag on 11/40 (mostly 1-2 min). The repair loop (`plan.length_problem`) only fires for too SHORT scripts (below 93%); nothing sends back an overlong one, and `plan.target_seconds` makes the timeline never shorter than asked, not longer. A 2-minute order can become about 2:20-2:50, and for a "30-60 second promo" request, 194 words is about 1:30.
* Cause: the prompt asks for 90-110% of words but the model overshoots small targets (minimum 3 beats x 20 words); `generate_plan` has no upper-bound check. (My speech estimate uses the repo's 2.4 words/s; I did not measure with TTS.)
* Fix: add an upper bound (>115%) to `length_problem` for the repair (M, 0.5 day); lower the per-beat minimum words for 1 minute videos.

### 3.5 Screen and narration mismatch (lists/timelines the narrator never reads)
* Evidence: 9/40 videos still carry the sync warning at the end ("some scenes list items their narration never mentions"), almost all in staged videos (4-5 min): `his-01` beat 14 timeline "6 April Siege begins / c. 22 April Ships hauled overland..." not narrated; `tec-03` beat 17 timeline "Sep 20 Training and evaluation paused"; `fin-03` beat 22; `kid-02`. Also 5/40 had scenes replaced for invalid props (`versus needs 2+ rows` appears 3 times; `bar_race`, `sequence`) and 3 had a kinetic_text swapped for a title card by the 3-per-video cap.
* Cause: in `staged.write_section` the coverage problem is returned as a `warning` only after one repair attempt, and `staged.generate` just appends it to warnings; non-staged `generate_plan` repairs it once. Section calls cannot see the other sections' scenes.
* Fix: second repair attempt for sections, or drop the list scene (turn it into `kinetic_text`) when still uncovered (M, 1 day). `versus`/chart props: one line in the scene docs or a pre-validation retry (L).

### 3.6 Formulaic endings and generic hooks
* Evidence: 24/40 closings begin with "So ...", "En resumen", "Recuerda", "The takeaway" ("So here is the takeaway.", "So remember one sentence."), 15/40 hooks are questions, and 19/40 open on a `title` scene. Judge hook/ending 3.23; weakest for trivia (2.0) and 1-minute videos (3.0). `how-01` "A flat tire never comes at a good time." is a bland opener (judge 2); `tri-03` opens "Space is stranger than any movie. Here are five weird facts."
* Cause: system prompt asks for hook and takeaway (`plan.system_prompt`, `staged.section_user`: "finish with a clear takeaway"), which the model reads as a template.
* Fix: add 2-3 contrasting examples and a ban list of closers ("So here is", "In summary") to the director craft; low effort, low-medium value (L-M, 0.5 day).

### 3.7 Visuals: variety fine, relevance and use of data weak
* Evidence: bullets 14% + kinetic_text 13% of all scenes; charts only 4 in 40 videos (1%), stat 4%, even for number-heavy topics (finance, news); `gallery`/`image` queries are written inside the script call with no check against the narration (judge visuals 3.21; typical defects: a diagram titled "How the lead was lost" in `new-01` although the leader won; truncated node labels; an empty-looking gallery in `sci-02`). Clips are disabled in web mode (`allow_clips = mode != "web"`), so news and current-event videos have fewer motion scenes.
* Cause: V3 hypotheses 8 (visual strategy chosen while writing).
* Fix: per-section visual strategy (V3 item 7) is a bigger change; the cheap part is a rule that any scene with 3+ numbers in the narration should be a chart/stat (L-M, 1 day). Unmeasured here: whether the stock photos actually match (no images were fetched).

### 3.8 Time-sensitive topics
* Evidence: of 6 time-sensitive prompts, 5 went to web research (new-01, new-02, new-03, tec-03, fin-03) and cited 4-11 sources. One was missed: `tra-03` ("...how the rail pass situation works now, cherry blossom timing, budget range") was classified encyclopedia, so the 4-minute outline was written from Wikipedia and only the sections did web searches; the result states a past year's forecast ("In 2026, the best viewing was forecast as March 28 to April 4 in Tokyo"), no yen budget figures although asked, and a price change as already in effect (judge accuracy 2). `new-01` named the right latest race (Las Vegas GP, 21 Nov 2026) but added unsupported details (3.2). 3 non-time-sensitive prompts also chose web (bio-02, cmp-02, tra-01), which is probably right for travel and product comparisons.
* Staged videos ALWAYS run web research per section when a provider exists (the `research_section` path ignores the top-level decision), so the label `mode=encyclopedia` is misleading for those 8 videos: sci-03 ran 11 searches, tra-03 10, his-02 11. That is why staged videos are better grounded and also why they cost $0.32 median.
* Fix: rank "current rules/prices/schedules/budget/forecast" as web triggers in `researcher.decide`; `tra-03` shows the LLM decider can miss, so also make the staged path honour a "current" cue (value M, effort 0.5 day).

### 3.9 Reliability
* One of 40 first attempts failed outright: `hea-03` (4 minutes, anxiety guide): `ValueError: section 1 could not be written: no JSON object in model output` (two consecutive unusable answers), $0.28 spent before the crash (`failed/hea-03.attempt1.json`). The identical prompt succeeded on the second try. 1 of 9 staged first attempts failed; in production this is a refund. Cause: `staged.write_section` retries once; I could not see the raw model text (not stored). Possibilities: refusal-like text for a mental-health topic, or output cut off. Fix: log the first 300 characters of an unparseable answer, one extra retry for sections, and fall back to the single-call writer (M, 1 day).

### 3.10 Spanish vs English
* Judge means are the same (es 3.32 vs en 3.26; language 3.92 vs 4.11; hook/ending 3.42 vs 3.11; accuracy 3.33 vs 3.07; ungrounded 10% vs 24%). Judge naturalness of the Spanish: 4 on 10 of 11 scripts, one 3 (`bio-02`), none 5 (judge is lenient on language; the owner's reading matters most here).
* Recurring Spanish defects found by the judge: tense switching inside historical narration in `his-02` ("Acusa a Madero... exige... termina con" mixed with preterite); regional vocabulary for a Spain viewer in `fin-02` ("canasta de bienes y servicios" instead of "cesta"); stiff connectors ("Veamos la energía.", "Entonces, la idea clave es esta" ); agreement errors read aloud in a maths lesson `kid-02` ("Uno cuarto más dos cuartos", "El numerador es las partes que tomas"); `cmp-02` "Un híbrido ronda entre cuatro coma cinco y cinco coma cinco litros" (unit missing). Sources for Spanish prompts come from es.wikipedia, which is shorter than en: `sci-02` cites 3 Spanish pages and its physics is imprecise ("Cada color es una onda de luz con distinto tamaño").
* Fix: a Spanish style block in the director prompt (consistent tense, region vocabulary from `region`, spell-out of numbers with units, no "Según las notas"); L-M, 0.5 day. Verify with the owner's naturalness scores first.

## 4. Cost and latency
* Plan stage per video: median $0.135, p90 $0.324, max $0.480 (`fin-03`, 5 min, 14 searches). By length: 1-2 min median $0.077 (p90 $0.114), 3 min $0.144 (p90 $0.190), 4-5 min $0.324 (p90 $0.415). Web videos median $0.190 (p90 $0.415), encyclopedia $0.114.
* Cost by part over the 40 runs: Sonnet script calls $5.44, search API $0.86, research agent (Haiku) $0.05, small Haiku calls $0.01. Research is cheap; the writer is the cost. Judge: $0.03 per video.
* Latency (plan stage, 3 videos in parallel so slightly pessimistic): median 41 s, p90 68 s, max 81 s. By length: 1-2 min 26 s (p90 39), 3 min 56 s (p90 70), 4-5 min 43 s (p90 69; sections run in parallel, so staged is not slower than 3 minutes). Web adds about 10-12 s before the first script call (`before_first_script_call_s` in each run).
* Upshot: there is room to spend more on research (about $0.05-0.10 per video) without moving the cost needle; the existing $0.06 per-plan guard in `eval_plans.py` is already exceeded by 33 of 40 videos (it is stale).

## 5. Verdict on the planner hypothesis (PIPELINE_V3)

What the data says: the weakest dimensions (coverage 2.80, originality 2.48, accuracy 3.15) are about **content depth and grounding**, not structure (3.42), tone (3.90) or language (4.03). The one structural intervention the pipeline already has (staged outline + research per section) is associated with the best coverage/originality/grounding. That supports building **deeper research and an outline for shorter videos**; it does not support a heavy new planner or intake for the categories that are already fine.

| Category (judge mean) | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| trivia / listicle (2.83), travel (2.89), biography (3.00), news (3.11), recipe (3.11), comparison (3.15), opinion (3.11), technology/AI (3.22) | **Would benefit** from deeper research + outline (planner lite) | coverage 2.0-2.7, originality 2.0-2.7; facts missing or bland; needs several sources, concrete numbers/quantities, a checklist of expected points |
| history (3.29), personal finance (3.33), how-to (3.33) | **Benefit from research depth and a grounding check**; outline already works when 4+ minutes | accuracy defects on specifics (Pacto de la Embajada, loan example, torque/steps) |
| news / current events | **Needs the web agent plus a grounding repair**, not a planner | research already web; the failure is invented details and thin context (no standings, no national positions) |
| concept explainer science (3.56), kids (3.62), productivity (3.73), marketing (3.62), fiction (3.50), health (3.67; n=1 completed fine, 1 crash) | **Fine as they are** for structure; add the no-invented-facts rule for marketing and health | scores are the best of the set; their problem is originality, which a planner would not fix; marketing needs a "use only the brief" rule instead |

Recommended order (value per effort, most to least): 3.1 (fetch failures, fallback to web, widened web routing; 1-2 days) -> 3.2 (rule + regex for "notes", then post-write grounding check; 0.5 + 3 days) -> re-run this audit (about $7) -> 3.3 (lower the staged threshold or planner lite) only if coverage on 1-3 minute videos is still below about 3.3 -> 3.4, 3.5, 3.9 (small fixes). The intake questions and parallel per-section agents (V3 items 8 and 9) are not supported or contradicted by this audit: ambiguity was not measured (all prompts were answered with a plausible reading; the vague ones, e.g. "explain entropy", "Historia de la Revolución Mexicana", worked well enough).

## 6. Limitations (what this audit cannot say)
* The judge is the same model family as the writer (Sonnet judging Sonnet; known self-preference), scores are harsh and absolute levels mean little (0/40 "would publish"). Single judgement per video, no repeat to measure variance. Its fact checking uses the notes plus its own knowledge, which can be older than November 2026; it was told to trust the notes for recent events. It cannot see or hear the final video (voice, pacing, image relevance, captions were not evaluated; 0 of 40 videos were rendered, whereas V3 proposed rendering 10).
* Correlation between my rule metric (ungrounded share) and the judge's accuracy is r = 0.05: the metric over-counts known facts and under-counts wrong claims about known entities.
* Sample size: 2-3 prompts per category; a category mean is an indication, not a statistic. The 4-5 minute vs 1-3 minute comparison is confounded by topic.
* Wikimedia 429s could not be separated from genuinely empty searches (3.1). One laptop IP, 3 parallel workers.
* One run per prompt: the writer is non-deterministic, so a single outlier (e.g. `tri-01` 2.44, lowest) may be partly luck. `hea-03` was run twice (first attempt crashed).
* Judge cost accounting: two scripts were re-judged after I fixed the judge prompt (props shown in full); the first judgement cost (about $0.07) is not in the saved files.
* The working tree had unrelated uncommitted edits in `backend/lambdas` (engine/plan.py, renderer.py, scenes, ...) made by someone else while the audit ran; the audit process imported the code at start, so every run used one consistent version, but I do not know which edits it included. The full backend suite finished with 1281 passed and 10 failed (5 in `test_el_photo_layouts.py`, `ValueError: Coordinate 'lower' is less than 'upper'` in PIL; others in `test_scene_styles.py` safe-area checks), all in scene rendering code that has those uncommitted edits; my 15 new tests pass and none of the failing tests touch a file I added.

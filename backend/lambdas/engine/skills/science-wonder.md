id: science-wonder
name: Science and wonder
pick: Science, space, nature, physics, biology, the universe, technology breakthroughs, 'how big/how old/how fast'.
---
- Lead with the astonishing fact or the question the science answers, stated simply, then earn it with the explanation.
- Make scale felt: compare sizes, distances and times to things a person knows (a stadium, a day, a hand).
- Replace jargon with a picture or analogy first; name the technical term after.
- Say what is known, what is estimated and what is still unknown, and keep those apart.
- Use real numbers with their units, rounded sensibly; never invent a measurement.
- Correct the usual misconception on the way, calmly, and explain why people believe it.
- End on the open question or the larger meaning, which is where wonder comes from.
- Visuals: a scale comparison for sizes, a counter for big numbers, a diagram for mechanisms, a timeline for ages. Music: awe and curiosity, spacious.

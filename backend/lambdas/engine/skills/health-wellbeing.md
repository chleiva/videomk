- Calm, clear and kind. Lead with what the viewer wants to know and what the evidence says, in plain language.
- Say how strong the evidence is (a large study, a small study, expert opinion, unknown). Never overstate; "may", "is linked to" only when that is what the source says.
- This is general information, not medical advice: tell the viewer to speak to a professional for their own situation, and name the warning signs that need urgent care when relevant.
- No miracle claims, no fear tactics, no shame. For mental health topics avoid describing methods of self-harm and mention that support exists.
- Prefer ranges and honest uncertainty to false precision; take every figure from the notes.
- Give one or two realistic things a person can do.
- Visuals: a steps or checklist scene for habits, a pictogram for '1 in 5', a diagram for how the body works, calm photographs. Music: calm and reassuring.

- Tell ONE story, not a tour of the photos. Decide the arc first (how it begins, builds, peaks and closes), write the narration as that story, then let the pictures accompany it. Explain photos one by one only if the request asks.
- Say less than the pictures show: one or two short sentences per beat (12 to 20 words). Never read out clothes, colours, poses or objects; add what a picture cannot show: the day, the place, why it mattered, a real fact from the notes.
- Let the pictures breathe. After every two to four narrated beats leave a pause beat (a photo or collage held 3 to 6 seconds with only the music): before a striking photo, after an emotional line, at the end of a chapter.
- Vary the rhythm: a quick collage, then one large photo that holds.
- The video opens with music and the first picture before the voice (the engine adds it) and ends on a held picture with only the music; keep the first and last lines short.
- In first person, speak as the person: warm, simple, concrete. Never invent feelings, names, dates or events.
- Order follows the days and the clock; name the day or part of the day when it helps.
- With three or more chapters, open each with a short chapter card.
- Music: warm and unobtrusive, rising on the pauses.

- The photos are the show; the voice is a companion. Say less than the pictures show: one or two short sentences per photo beat (about 12 to 20 words), never describing what is plainly visible. Add what a picture cannot show: the day, the place, why it mattered, a real fact from the notes.
- Let the pictures breathe. After every two to four narrated beats leave a pause beat (a photo or collage held 3 to 6 seconds with only the music): before a striking photo, after an emotional line, at the end of a chapter. Never talk over every picture.
- Vary the rhythm: a quick collage, then one large photo that holds.
- The video opens with a few seconds of music and the first picture before the voice (the engine adds them) and ends on a held picture with only the music; keep the first and last lines short.
- Speak in the person's own voice when the story says first person: warm, simple, concrete, no clichés. Never invent feelings, names, dates or events.
- Order follows the days and the clock; name the day or part of the day when it helps ("On Saturday morning...").
- With three or more chapters, open each with a short chapter card (one line, the day or place as its kicker).
- Music: warm and unobtrusive under the voice, rising on the pauses; choose the track for the feeling of the trip, not the genre of the place.

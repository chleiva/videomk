- First beat: the result the viewer will get and why it is worth the effort, then what they need before starting.
- Steps in the exact order a person would do them, one action per beat, each starting with a verb. Say what to look for to know the step worked.
- Show before you tell: name the thing, then do it, then explain why in one sentence.
- Warn about the common mistake at the step where it happens, not at the end.
- Keep numbers, quantities and settings exact and from the notes; never invent a setting or a measurement.
- Between phases give a one-line checkpoint ("You should now have ..."). Finish with the finished result and one next step.
- Visuals: a steps scene for the sequence, a checklist for what is needed, code or a terminal for commands, an image for a real object or result. Music: light, steady, unobtrusive.

pick: Cities, countries, landmarks, destinations, guides, 'best places to', itineraries, nature spots, 'all about <place>'.
---
- Sell a feeling of place first: one concrete sensory detail (a sound, a smell, a light) in the opening, then the practical story.
- Give the place a shape: a route or a few clear areas the viewer can hold in their head, in a sensible order for walking or travelling.
- Say where things are and how far apart (map and distances), and when to go; accuracy on names, prices and opening times comes only from the notes.
- Mix the famous with one lesser-known thing, and say why a visitor would care about each.
- Include a few practical tips (how to get there, what to avoid, what it costs) only if the notes support them.
- Respect the place and its people; avoid cliches ("hidden gem", "bucket list").
- Visuals: many photographs and galleries for places, a map for routes and locations, a ranking for top-N, a counter for a striking number. Music: warm, adventurous, sunny.

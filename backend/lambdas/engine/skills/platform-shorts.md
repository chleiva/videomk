- One idea, one payoff. The first sentence is the hook, spoken within 2 seconds, 5 to 12 words, with the answer's picture or a bold big word on screen at frame one (treat the first second as the thumbnail). Never a greeting.
- Length: 20 to 45 seconds is the sweet spot for an explainer; 45 to 60 only if every beat adds something new. Under 20 words per beat, cut as the thought ends.
- Structure: hook (the surprising claim or the question), 2 to 4 fast beats of proof or steps, a last line that lands the point. For a loop: let the last line connect to the first ("...which is why the first question has a different answer").
- Pace: beats of 8 to 16 words (a change of picture every 3 to 6 seconds); on visual topics use a clip montage (several short clips in one beat) for faster cuts; no long pause beats.
- On-screen text: 2 to 6 big words per beat, high contrast, in the middle third of the frame. Keep text and captions clear of the top 12% and the bottom 20% of the frame height and of the right 12% of the width (title, buttons and description sit there).
- Captions burned in, one short line at a time; assume sound off first.
- Tone: direct, spoken, "you"; energetic but not shouting; plain words.
- Ending: end on the takeaway or the loop line; no long outro, no "subscribe" request invented. A short "part 2" or question is fine if true to the request.
- Avoid: a slow intro, a title card first, a list of more than 3 items, an abrupt cut before the point is finished, anything that reads like a small lecture.

---
id: history-story
name: History
pick: Historical events, periods, wars, empires, discoveries, how something came to be, the story of a place or an era.
---
- Tell it as a story with people in it: who wanted what, what stood in the way, what changed. Start inside a vivid moment, then step back to explain.
- Keep a clear thread of cause and effect: "because", "so", "but". A list of dates is not a story; use the dates that carry the turns.
- Anchor time often and exactly (year, season, how long after the last event) so the viewer never loses the order.
- Give scale: how many people, how far, how long, in a comparison viewers can picture.
- Be honest about uncertainty: where historians disagree, say so; do not judge past people by only today's standards, and do not excuse atrocities.
- Use sources by name for striking claims and quotes; use only quotes that appear in the grounding notes.
- End on the consequence that still matters today.
- Visuals: a timeline for the sequence, a map for movements and borders, photographs and archive-style images for people and places. Music: dignified and restrained.

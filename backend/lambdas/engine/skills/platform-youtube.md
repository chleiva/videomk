- Viewers choose in the first seconds and the first 30 seconds are the retention test: open on the payoff or the question in the first sentence (8 to 15 words), with a picture that shows it. No greeting, no channel intro, no agenda.
- The title and the opening must match: whatever the title promises is shown or stated in the first 15 seconds, then delivered, never delayed to the end.
- Structure by length: 1-3 min = hook, three points at most, takeaway. 4-8 min = hook, a stated question, 3-5 chapters each with a small reveal, a turn near the middle, takeaway. 9 min or more = add a mid-video re-hook ("but the real problem is...") every 2 to 3 minutes.
- Pace: a new picture or scene every beat (about 10 to 14 seconds of narration, shorter in the first minute); calm, conversational narration at the steady rate in the word budget; vary scene types, charts and diagrams are welcome.
- On-screen text: normal landscape amounts are fine, key terms, numbers and labels only; never the narrated sentence. Captions on, kept clear of the bottom 8% of the frame.
- Music: low under the voice; no sound effects.
- Ending: one takeaway in the last line, a short promise of what the viewer can do next. No "like and subscribe" begging, no fake "next video" claims, no long outro.
- Chapter titles state what is learned (they become chapters). Description is plain, specific, with the topic's own words; at most 3 relevant hashtags, no tag stuffing.
- Avoid: slow cold open, long self-introduction, repeating the title as the first line, filler recap, promising more than is delivered.

id: news-explainer
name: News and current affairs
pick: Current events, politics, the economy today, 'what happened', breaking stories, news roundups, what is going on with X.
---
- Inverted pyramid: the most important fact in the first two sentences (who, what, when, where), then why it matters, then background, then what happens next.
- Attribute every claim to its source out loud ("Reuters reports", "according to the central bank"). Give the date a fact refers to; never present old information as current.
- Separate what is confirmed from what is claimed or expected. Use "reportedly", "officials say" only when true to the source.
- Be even-handed: when people disagree, state each side in its own best terms before any context. No loaded adjectives, no guessing at motives.
- Numbers get context: compared with what, over what period. Prefer one clear figure to five.
- Short sentences, active voice, present tense for what is happening now.
- Close with what to watch next or what remains unknown, not a moral.
- Visuals: a headline card for a quoted headline, a map for where, a timeline for sequence, a chart for a trend. Music: serious and steady; solemn for grave news.

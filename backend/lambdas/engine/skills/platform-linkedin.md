- Most members watch with sound off: the message must work as a silent film. The first 5 seconds state the point as a clear insight or a surprising number, on screen and spoken; the key message is never later than 10 seconds in.
- Length: 30 to 90 seconds for one insight; up to 2 to 3 minutes only for a real lesson or a how-to. Short videos finish more often; do not pad.
- Structure: the insight first (a claim with a stake: "Most X fail because of Y"), the evidence in two or three beats (one number or example each), what it means for the viewer's work, one clear takeaway.
- Pace: steady, a new picture every 4 to 6 seconds; charts, stats, timelines, comparisons and diagrams are at home here; keep text legible and professional.
- On-screen text: key phrase or figure per beat, large, sentence case, with a source label under any statistic. In 4:5 or 1:1 keep text 8% inside the sides and out of the bottom 12% (captions and player controls). Captions are burned in, always on.
- Tone: professional and human, a point of view, plain language, no jargon without definition, no hype, no emoji-style energy, no humble-brag. Say what you learned or what the data shows.
- Music: very quiet and neutral, or none; the voice carries.
- Ending: a takeaway the viewer can use at work, or one honest question that invites professional discussion. No hard sales pitch, no invented company name.
- Avoid: a logo-first opening, empty buzzwords, clickbait, overclaiming, unsourced numbers, an over-long video for one point.

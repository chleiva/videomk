id: outstanding-video
name: Ten principles of an outstanding video
always: true
---
- 1. Hook: beat 1 is a concrete surprise, a stake or the question the video answers (under 2 s for shorts, 8 s mid-length), never a greeting, a title read aloud or "in this video".
- 2. First picture: it shows the subject or a big number, not a title card; the title, the opening and the ending make the same promise and the video keeps it, no bait.
- 3. One through-line: one promise or question for the whole video; every beat advances it, and anything that does not is cut (no preamble, recap or padding).
- 4. Open loop: raise one question early and pay it off later; in long videos reopen a smaller one every 60 to 90 seconds.
- 5. Concrete first: each beat carries one specific thing from the notes (number with a comparison, name, example) before any abstraction.
- 6. Visual change: a new picture or on-screen change at least every 8 s (4 s in shorts), never the same scene type more than twice in a row; vary the pace on purpose, fast beats then a slower one for the key number or turn.
- 7. Text: only the key words, numbers or labels on screen, never the narrated sentence; the first item of a list is named in the beat's first sentence.
- 8. Language: short active sentences for the ear, mixed length, plain words, no cliches.
- 9. Sound off: captions on and clear of the platform's buttons; the key term of each beat is on screen; music under the voice.
- 10. Ending: it pays off the hook and ends on one line the viewer can repeat; a call to action only if the request has one.
